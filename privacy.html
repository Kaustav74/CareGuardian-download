CareGuardian Privacy Policy

Effective date: 19 October 2026 · App: CareGuardian (Android package in.careguardian.app) and its web version

CareGuardian is a health-care coordination app run by Kaustav Nath ("we", "us"), based in India. It is used by patients and their family members, and by health-care workers and administrators in 11 other roles: doctor, nurse, receptionist, hospital admin, pharmacist, diagnostics staff, ambulance crew, emergency operator, rural volunteer, network admin and super admin. This policy explains what personal data we collect, why, who processes it, how long we keep it and what your rights are under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act).

1. Who can use CareGuardian

You must be 18 or older to create an account. A family member or carer aged 18+ may manage information about a minor or dependent with that person's or their lawful guardian's permission.

CareGuardian is a coordination tool. It is not an emergency service, it does not dispatch ambulances, and it has no partnership or integration with any hospital, ambulance operator, AIIMS, ABDM/ABHA or any government system. In an emergency, call 112 (or 108 for an ambulance) directly.

3. Consent (DPDP Act)

We process your personal data on the basis of your consent, which you give at sign-up after reading an itemised notice. Health data, location, camera/microphone and the AI Medical Explainer each ask for your agreement before first use, and Android asks separately for device permissions. You can withdraw consent at any time in Settings or by writing to the Grievance Officer; withdrawal does not affect processing already done, but some features will stop working.

4. Who can see your data

  • Your care team: clinical and hospital staff in CareGuardian only see records they need for their role, as enforced by role-based access controls on the server and database. Family members only see data for members who have linked them.
  • Supabase Inc. (data processor) hosts our database, sign-in and file storage in its Mumbai, India region.
  • Vercel Inc. (data processor) hosts the CareGuardian website and web app and processes standard request logs (such as IP address) to serve and secure it.
  • Groq, Inc. (USA) processes AI Medical Explainer messages to generate replies. Zero Data Retention is enabled, so Groq does not store your messages, and they are not used to train models. This is the only feature that sends the content you type outside India. Do not type information you don't want processed this way.
  • Video calls use a public STUN server (Google) only to connect the two devices; the audio and video itself goes directly between participants.
  • Map tiles are loaded from OpenStreetMap, which sees your IP address and the map area requested, but not your account.
  • Authorities, where Indian law requires it.

5. Retention and deletion

  • You can delete your account in the app (Profile → Delete account) or by emailing the Grievance Officer. We delete your account and personal data within 30 days, and it is removed from backups within a further 30 days.
  • If a doctor or other staff member deletes their account, clinical records they wrote for other patients are kept as part of those patients' care history, with the author's name removed.
  • Accounts inactive for 3 years are removed.
  • Some records may need to be kept longer where the law requires (for example, audit logs a hospital must keep); we tell you if this applies.

6. Security

Data is encrypted in transit (HTTPS) and at rest, access is restricted by role at both the app and database level, and access to health records is logged.

7. Your rights

Under the DPDP Act you can ask to access, correct, complete or erase your data, withdraw consent, nominate someone to act for you, and raise a grievance. If you are not satisfied with our response you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

8. Not medical advice

CareGuardian's clinical-role features (prescriptions, nursing timelines, diagnostics, alerts and dictation) are tools for qualified professionals, who remain responsible for their clinical decisions. The AI Medical Explainer and patient-facing information are not a substitute for professional medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.
